REVENUE MODELS AND BUSINESS MODEL CANVAS GUIDE

=== REVENUE MODELS ===

1. SUBSCRIPTION (SaaS/Recurring Revenue)
   Best for: Software, content, services
   Examples: Netflix, Spotify, Salesforce, Slack
   Variants:
   - Freemium: Free basic tier + paid premium ($10-$100/month)
   - Tiered pricing: Starter/Pro/Enterprise ($0/$49/$199/month)
   - Usage-based: Pay per API call, per GB, per transaction
   - Seat-based: Per user per month
   Key metrics: MRR, ARR, Churn rate (<5% monthly is good), LTV:CAC ratio (>3x)

2. MARKETPLACE/PLATFORM
   Best for: Two-sided or multi-sided platforms
   Examples: Airbnb (3-15% take rate), Uber (20-25%), eBay (10-12%)
   Revenue: Commission, listing fees, featured placements, subscriptions
   Key metrics: GMV, take rate, liquidity (supply/demand balance)

3. TRANSACTIONAL / E-COMMERCE
   Best for: Physical or digital product sales
   Examples: Amazon, Shopify stores, D2C brands
   Revenue: Product margin (20-60%), cross-sell, upsell
   Key metrics: AOV, conversion rate, repeat purchase rate, ROAS

4. ADVERTISING
   Best for: High-traffic media, apps, platforms
   Examples: Google, Facebook, free apps
   Revenue: CPM ($1-10), CPC ($0.10-$5), CPA (% of sale)
   Key metrics: DAU/MAU, time on platform, CPM rates, fill rate

5. LICENSING
   Best for: IP, technology, content, brands
   Examples: IBM IP licensing, music licensing, franchise models
   Revenue: Upfront fee + royalty (2-10% of revenue)

6. PROFESSIONAL SERVICES / CONSULTING
   Best for: B2B specialized expertise
   Revenue: Hourly ($50-500/hr), retainer, project-based
   Key metrics: Utilization rate, billable hours, project margin

7. DATA/API MONETIZATION
   Best for: Data aggregators, analytics platforms
   Examples: Bloomberg, credit bureaus, weather APIs
   Revenue: Per API call, data subscription, insights reports

8. HARDWARE + RAZOR-BLADE MODEL
   Best for: IoT, consumer hardware
   Examples: Printer + ink, Keurig + pods, Tesla + software
   Revenue: Low margin hardware + high margin consumables/services

9. FRANCHISE MODEL
   Best for: Proven business concepts with replication potential
   Examples: McDonald's, Subway, Anytime Fitness
   Revenue: Franchise fee ($10K-$45K) + ongoing royalties (4-8%)

10. PAY-PER-USE / ON-DEMAND
    Best for: Infrastructure, utilities, professional services
    Examples: AWS, cloud services, gig economy
    Revenue: Directly tied to consumption

=== BUSINESS MODEL CANVAS COMPONENTS ===

KEY PARTNERS:
- Strategic alliances, joint ventures
- Supplier relationships
- Technology partners (IBM Cloud, AWS, Google Cloud)
- Channel partners and resellers
- Questions: Who are key partners? What do we get/give in partnerships?

KEY ACTIVITIES:
- Core value-creating activities
- Production, problem-solving, platform management
- Questions: What key activities does our value proposition require?

KEY RESOURCES:
- Physical: Manufacturing plants, vehicles, machines
- Intellectual: IP, patents, brand, databases
- Human: Experts, creative teams, sales force
- Financial: Cash, credit lines, stock options

VALUE PROPOSITIONS:
- Novelty, performance, customization, convenience
- Design, brand/status, price, cost reduction
- Risk reduction, accessibility, usability
- Questions: What value do we deliver? What problems do we solve?

CUSTOMER RELATIONSHIPS:
- Personal assistance, self-service, automated services
- Communities, co-creation
- Questions: What type of relationship does each customer segment expect?

CHANNELS:
- Awareness, evaluation, purchase, delivery, after-sales
- Own channels vs. partner channels
- Direct: website, in-house sales; Indirect: distributors, retail

CUSTOMER SEGMENTS:
- Mass market, niche market, segmented, diversified, multi-sided platforms
- Questions: For whom are we creating value? Who are our most important customers?

COST STRUCTURE:
- Fixed costs: rent, salaries, equipment
- Variable costs: raw materials, commissions
- Economies of scale and scope
- Cost-driven vs. value-driven

REVENUE STREAMS:
- Asset sale, usage fee, subscription, lending/renting/leasing
- Licensing, brokerage, advertising

=== UNIT ECONOMICS BENCHMARKS ===

SaaS:
- LTV:CAC ratio > 3x (healthy), > 5x (excellent)
- CAC Payback Period: <12 months (good), <6 months (excellent)
- Gross Margin: 60-80% (good), 80%+ (excellent)
- Net Revenue Retention: >100% (expansion revenue, excellent)

E-commerce:
- Gross Margin: 30-50%
- CAC: $20-80 depending on channel
- Repeat purchase rate: >30% (healthy)
- ROAS (Return on Ad Spend): >3x

Marketplace:
- Take rate: 5-20% depending on vertical
- GMV growth: >100% YoY for early-stage
- Liquidity score: >60% match rate

=== PRICING STRATEGIES ===

Cost-plus pricing: Cost × (1 + margin%)
Competitive pricing: Match or beat market rates
Value-based pricing: Price based on customer perceived value (best for B2B SaaS)
Penetration pricing: Low initial price to gain market share
Freemium: Free core + paid upgrades (conversion rate 2-5%)
Price anchoring: Present expensive option to make target price seem reasonable

=== FINANCIAL PROJECTIONS FRAMEWORK ===

Year 1 Focus: Product-market fit, first 100 customers
Year 2 Focus: Scalable acquisition channels, unit economics
Year 3 Focus: Expansion, international, new product lines

Typical SaaS Expense Breakdown (early stage):
- R&D: 35-45% of revenue
- Sales & Marketing: 30-40% of revenue
- G&A: 15-20% of revenue
- Target: Gross margin positive by Month 12-18
